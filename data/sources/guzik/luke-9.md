# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (English page), Luke 9
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/luke-9/ . Our own outline, not his text. (Arabic page arabic.enduringword.com/ar/luke-09-ar/ redirects to the Arabic index; no Arabic chapter page readable.)
A1 (1-2): power and authority with the task; whom God calls He equips. Clarke: demons and diseases distinct, cast out vs healed. Pate: wordplay apostello/apostles. Content of preaching: King arrived, different kingdom, repentant community; healing tied to preaching. Luke 6:12-16.
A2 (3-6): travel light, dependence; Barclay: Talmud rule on Temple Mount (staff, shoes, money bag, dusty feet). Liefeld: bag of itinerant philosophers/beggars. Pate: Josephus on Essenes. Shaking dust = treat as Gentile city; task to present, not force.
A3 (7-9): Herod perplexed: rumours + guilty conscience (Matt 14:1-12); John, Elijah (Mal 4:5-6), prophet (Deut 18:15-19); curiosity or threat; "that fox" Luke 13:32; danger growing.
A4 (10): Jesus cares about results; withdraws to bless servants.
B1 (11): receives crowds, teaches kingdom, heals (Matt 14:14).
B2 (12-15): disciples want to send away; "you give them"; groups of fifty, orderly banquet, reliable count.
B3 (16-17): thanks to God not blessing the food; authority over creation through disciples' hands; 2 Kgs 4:42-44; John 6:8-9; Morgan: servants yield all to Christ and obey despite worldly wisdom; all filled, surplus.
C1 (18-20): crowds then personal question; Morgan: Jesus prayed apart, not with disciples; "Christ of God" not political messiah.
C2 (21-22): silence until they grasp suffering Messiah; Barclay: learn meaning before preaching; "must" = God's plan; 1 Pet 1:20; Rev 13:8; resurrection as necessary.
C3 (23): "to all"; cross = death, no way back; voluntary unlike Rome's; daily = spiritual; self-denial = others-centred life.
C4 (24-27): losing life is only route to life; Luke 4:5-8; future life implied; shame from fear, social pressure, intellectual pride; hope of seeing the kingdom.
D1 (28-29): prayer setting; glory just after cross talk; pause in ongoing miracle of veiled glory; Matt 17:2; Pate: Tabor tradition via Origen, others Hermon.
D2 (30-31): recognition in heaven; Law and Prophets; Moses = die and glorified, Elijah = caught up (Jude 9; 2 Kgs 2:11; 1 Thess 4:13-18); possibly two witnesses Rev 11:3-13; Meyer: how Adam's race might have entered heaven without death; Pate: suffering path to glory; Trapp: decease = exodus.
D3 (32): sleep, saw glory when fully awake; Spurgeon: focus on Jesus' glow; Jesus praying when transfigured.
D4 (33-34): stay in glory, avoid cross; Jesus on level with Moses/Elijah; Shekinah cloud; "overshadow" Luke 1:35; Spurgeon: real vision not a dream.
D5 (35-36): beloved Son, hear Him; Moses/Elijah subordinate; 2 Pet 1:16-18; John 1:14; new birth greater miracle.
E1 (37-40): descent to demonic trouble; Morgan: mountain glory vs valley failure; epilepsy-like but demonic; Luke 9:1; Clarke: real cause lack of faith; Matt 17:21; Eph 6:12.
E2 (41-42): faithless generation; Barclay: "threw down" = boxer's knockout/wrestler; Spurgeon "The Devil's Last Throw": Satan strikes hardest as people come ("not elect", "too late").
E3 (43-45): amazed at majesty but Jesus refocuses on betrayal; Luke 24:6-8; Clarke: His sufferings should occupy our meditation; Trapp: besotted with earthly kingdom.
F1 (46-48): jealousy after mountain (Barclay citing Bede); child; receiving child = receiving Jesus; Spurgeon: own advancement vs His abasement; Pate: reversal; Luke 22:24.
F2 (49-50): exclusivity rebuked; Luke 9:40; Phil 1:15-18.
F3 (51-53): set face = courage (Isa 50:7); "received up" threefold; Barclay: route/hospitality unusual; Clarke: messengers probably James and John; Pate: Samaritans, Gerizim, John Hyrcanus; Spurgeon sermon "The Redeemer's Face Set like a Flint".
F4 (54-56): Boanerges Mark 3:17; Rom 12:19; Trapp: consult Christ before revenge; Clarke: followers of crucified never avenge; Wesley via Barclay: against party bigotry.
F5 (57-58): honest cost; Carson: homeless not penniless; Spurgeon: Jesus could close the door.
F6 (59-60): indefinite delay; quality over quantity; Morrison: torn between two rights.
F7 (61-62): short delay fatal; Spurgeon: farewells ruined many; Barclay: ploughman looking back; Morrison: ploughman valued for holding on.
