# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), Acts 17
Read 2026-11-27 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/acts-17/ (Arabic page exists). Our own outline, not his text. Headings print verse ranges reversed (RTL); normalized here. The page labels the Athens part "ثانياً" a second time (should be third); registered as printed with [كذا].
A1 (1-4): Thessalonica major port ~160 km from Philippi, ~3 days; still a big city; custom of synagogue; three Sabbaths; Hughes on "reasoned" (dialogue, Q&A), "opening" (lit. open), "demonstrating" (lay out evidence); Messiah must suffer and rise, Jesus is he; focus on who Jesus is; some Jews, many devout Greeks, leading women; Phil 4:15-16 support.
A2 (5-8): envy; marketplace worthless men; Jason's house; parallels 13:45,50; 14:2,5; 14:19; "turned world upside down" unintended compliment; Luke 12:16-21 rich fool; world already upside down, God sets it right; gospel makes better citizens, prayers for rulers; Longenecker: Paul avoids "king/kingdom" language in letters to avoid sedition misreading; crowd and rulers troubled.
A3 (9-10a): bond from Jason; Romans indifferent to beliefs but harsh for order; Jason paid though innocent; sent away by night not to endanger church/bond; few weeks only, led to 1 Thessalonians, among earliest letters.
B1 (10b-12): Bereans more noble: received eagerly + searched daily; Paul (13 NT books) still tested, testing teachers by Scripture healthy; open heart + clear mind (many have clear minds, closed hearts); consistent search; Scripture full of truth not mere poetry; Greek women of standing, men.
B2 (13-15): Thessalonian Jews pursue; fifth city fled (Antioch, Iconium, Lystra, Thessalonica, Berea); Silas and Timothy left: concern for churches not just converts; ministry not on Paul alone.
C1 (16-17): spirit stirred; kateidolos = saturated with idols; beauty not glorifying God did not impress him; synagogue + agora daily; Athens like Oxford/Cambridge; Bruce: lost political power, still peak of culture; Williams: capital of wisdom and religious capital of Greece.
C2 (18-21): Epicureans (pleasure, peace, gods unconcerned, remove fear of death) and Stoics (duty, fate, all is divine, accept what happens); "babbler"; foreign deities: Jesus and resurrection; Areopagus, new teaching; Athenians only telling/hearing new; Adam Clarke on London's appetite for news, preachers reading Scripture like newspapers.
C3 (22-23): very religious not necessarily praise, religion can keep from God; begins not with Scripture but with their religion; unknown god altars from fear of neglecting a god; Epimenides plague and sheep story ~600 years before.
C4 (24-29): Creator vs Epicurean chance and Stoic pantheism (Stott: must change their idea of God); God needs nothing, gives life/breath; one blood, times and boundaries; seek, not far; poets: Epimenides (also Titus 1:12) and Aratus (~310 BC), quoted as bridge reflecting biblical truth not as prophets; offspring so not gold/silver/stone; Stott: altar confesses ignorance, Paul holds them responsible.
C5 (30-31): repent all everywhere; not a soft message, confronts with judgment; first mention of Jesus, as judge; Paul probably interrupted; resurrection the proof for all; Christianity meaningless without it.
C6 (32-34): Greeks: immortal soul, body inferior (Boice); mock / hear later; Paul leaves, wanted to speak of Jesus; Dionysius, Damaris, others; critics: no cross, no OT, compromise; Ramsay: disappointment led to cross-centred Corinth (1 Cor 1:18-2:5); Bruce: biblical, from Creator to Judge; Stott: no gospel without doctrine of God, cross without creation, salvation without judgment; Longenecker: poor response reflects Athenian hearts; recorded speech a summary (~2 minutes).
