# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic), Mark 2
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/mark-2/ . Our own outline, not his text.
1A (1-4): crowd filled the house and door; Stedman: crowds pressed for healing and hindered his teaching; roofs of thatch and mud; Morgan: they dug through, not merely uncovered; determination and faith of the friends.
1B (5-7): he saw their faith and declared sins forgiven; the friends came for the body, Jesus met the deeper need; walking legs are no use to one going to ruin; not that this sickness came from a particular sin, but the fallen nature behind all suffering; Wiersbe: forgiveness the greatest miracle (greatest need, highest cost, eternal results); scribes' logic right (only God forgives), their error was rejecting who he is; Bruce: from wonder to doubt to accusation; Cole: if not God then blasphemer, no third option.
1C (8-12): knew their thoughts (Trapp: by deity or by gift, both possible); which is easier: forgiveness unseen, healing seen, he accepts the test; Barclay: rabbis held sickness proved unforgiven sin, so healing proved forgiveness; Son of Man = Dan 7:13-14 king and judge; Robertson: messianic but without political overtones; everyone tense, Jesus at peace; his word suffices; crowd glorified God.
2A (13-14): kept teaching (1:38); Levi = Matthew (Matt 9:9); tax collectors hated, worked for Rome, extorted; Lane: socially excluded, barred from testifying, shame on family; bought collection contracts; Wessel: fastest way to wealth, so leaving it was costly.
2B (15-17): reclined with sinners, perhaps a farewell feast; table = friendship, a scandal; Pharisees = the separated ones; physician saying; Jesus the great physician of the soul: always available, exact diagnosis, full cure, pays the fee himself.
3A (18-20): Pharisees fasted twice a week (Luke 18:12); God not against fasting but Jesus did not make it a measure of piety; rabbis ranked wedding joy above some duties; Clarke: weddings were loud celebrations; Jesus the Messiah-bridegroom, joy where he is; fasting after he is taken (ascension).
3B (21-22): patch and wineskins, the new tears the old; fermenting wine bursts old skins; Jesus replaced fasting with feasting, sackcloth with the robe of righteousness, despair with praise, law with grace; rigid forms seldom hold the Spirit's work; acorn image: not abolishing but fulfilling.
3C (23-24): plucking allowed (Deut 23:25), the issue was the Sabbath; rabbis counted it as reaping, threshing, winnowing, preparing food; detailed rules on carrying and knots; Jesus broke human additions, not God's command.
3D (25-28): David and the showbread (1 Sam 21:1-6): human need above ritual; Sabbath made for man (Morgan: using it against man contradicts God's purpose); Hos 6:6; Isa 58; Abiathar problem: possible solutions (both held office, in his time, or the passage known by his name; Wiersbe: passages named by main figure); Son of Man Lord of the Sabbath.
