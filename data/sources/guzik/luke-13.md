# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (English page), Luke 13
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/luke-13/ . Our own outline, not his text. No Arabic page found by search (arabic.enduringword.com/ar/luke-13-ar/ redirects to the Arabic index).
A1 (1-5): no secular record of the Galilean incident; Barclay on Pilate's aqueduct riot (similar, not same); man-made atrocity plus natural accident; Jesus denies "worse sinners" link but does not call victims innocent; Spurgeon: godly also die in disasters; shift from "why" to "what does it mean for me"; two tenses of repentance (v3 continuing, v5 once-for-all); fulfilled in Jerusalem's fall; Barclay: national sin brings national suffering.
A2 (6-9): God seeks fruit; fruit of Spirit (Gal 5:22-23); patience and extra care (manure); justice, a day of reckoning; Spurgeon: time to fell fruitless trees; Barclay's four lessons (uselessness invites disaster, only taking cannot survive, second chance, final chance).
B1 (10-13): still in synagogues; spiritual cause though not all illness is spiritual; Pate citing J. Wilkinson (spondylitis ankylopoetica); Clarke: painful and shameful; Spurgeon: 18 years not seeing the sky; Guzik denies this proves believers can be possessed (she was before the cross; Christians are new creatures); Spurgeon: Satan tied her like a beast; Jesus' touch as sympathy (Spurgeon); she kept attending synagogue.
B2 (14): Clarke: the demon went into the ruler's heart; ruler had no power to heal any day; Barclay: he spoke to the crowd, not to Jesus.
B3 (15-17): hypocrite; ox and donkey argument; Pate: wordplay "loose"; four reasons she deserved mercy (woman, daughter of Abraham, bound by Satan, 18 years); strong "must"; Spurgeon: Jesus knew her case; adversaries shamed, crowd rejoiced.
C1 (18-19): Guzik departs from traditional growth reading: abnormal growth, birds as Satan's agents (Matt 13:4, 19); Carson: birds = evil in Jewish literature (Rev 18:2); Morgan: corrupting elements shelter in Christianity; Dan 4:10-16; applies to Christianized Rome.
C2 (20-21): leaven = corruption (Exod 12:8, 15-20); Barclay: shock; France: about 40 litres, bread for ~100; hiding leaven offensive; Morgan: "paganizing influences".
D1 (22-24a): journey; Jesus redirects to one's own salvation; Pate: rabbinic debate all vs few; Clarke: curiosity; strive = agonize; Bruce: prize-fight; Trapp: Olympic garland; not works, Jesus is the gate; obstacles world, devil, flesh.
D2 (24b-27): urgency, door shut; Spurgeon: seeking vs striving; Morgan: limits to divine mercy; familiarity is not relationship; workers of iniquity.
D3 (28-30): hell incl. seeing others enter; toothless anecdote; Carson: weeping = suffering, gnashing = despair; Spurgeon: Jesus spoke most of hell; Gentiles from four directions; heaven: rest, company, all nations, certain; Spurgeon on knowing one another; France: radical change; Barclay and Spurgeon on reversal (wonder of grace / wonder of sin).
D4 (31-33): not all Pharisees hostile; Barclay's seven Pharisees from Talmud; Pate: divine schedule; Geldenhuys: fox = cunning but weak, not lion; Barclay on fox; Herod "first who will be last"; perfected = reach goal (Clarke), Morgan: third day points to cross; Spurgeon: probably a proverb; Morgan: quiet fearlessness.
D5 (34-35): repeated name (Luke 10:41; Acts 9:4); love aware of sin; France: OT bird imagery (Ps 17:8; 91:4; Isa 31:5); Clarke: hen calls chicks when predator near, Rome's eagle coming; Morgan: "mother heart of God"; "how often" hints at John's earlier visits; unwillingness was Jerusalem's; Pate: AD 70; Clarke: after fulness of Gentiles (Rom 11:26).
