# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic), Mark 1
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/mark-1/ . Our own outline, not his text.

1 (intro): church fathers linked the four Gospels to the four faces (Rev 4:7); Mark = the ox, Jesus the Servant; "immediately" 40+ times; Mark shows Jesus acting more than speaking (cites A. T. Robertson). Peter as Mark's source (1 Pet 5:13 "my son"); Mark's failure and restoration (2 Tim 4:11); vivid eyewitness details and Aramaic words (Robertson). Written early, probably in Rome, for Romans; no genealogy for a servant; Latin loanwords. Mark perhaps the young man of 14:51-52; church met in his mother's house (Acts 12:12).
2A (1-5): "gospel" = good news; Lane: Roman use for an emperor's birth (Augustus inscription). Mal 3:1 + Isa 40:3; first prophetic word after long silence. Preparing the road for a king, the real preparation is in the heart (cites Stedman). John's baptism: full immersion (Barclay); Jews baptized Gentile converts, so a Jew baptized confessed he was as needy as a Gentile; Christian baptism goes further (Rom 6:3). Many came because they knew their sin; the main message: Messiah is coming; repentance is the fitting response.
2B (6-8): John like Elijah (2 Kgs 1:8); always pointed away from himself; Lane on the Talmud: a disciple did every service for his rabbi except untying sandals, John unworthy even of that; water baptism prepares for Jesus' baptism with the Spirit, water alone does not cleanse.
2C (9-11): Jesus baptized not to be cleansed but to identify with sinners; humble setting (common name, despised Nazareth, Galilee, Jordan not used for purification per Lane); Bruce: heavens torn like a curtain; Spirit "like a dove" (Luke 3:22) not a dove; dove recalls Gen 1:2, gentleness; Trinity together; four witnesses: Mark, prophets, John, the Father.
2D (12-13): "drove" = same verb Jesus uses for casting out demons (Morgan); Heb 4:15; Mark gives no details, Jesus tested through the whole period; forty = testing (flood, wilderness, Moses); with the beasts in peace, like Adam before the fall, authority over creation (Trapp); angels served him (Morgan).
3A (14a): John's imprisonment told in 6:17-28; Galilee large and populous (Josephus' village figures; Guzik estimates over three million).
3B (14b-15): kingdom of God different from political hopes; kairos vs chronos; repentance = change of direction (New York to Los Angeles illustration); believe (pisteuo) = trust, not mere assent (Morgan).
3C (16-20): not their first meeting (John 1:35-42); ordinary fishermen chosen for what he would make of them; Wiersbe on fishermen's patience and teamwork; "follow me" = the heart of Christianity, teacher-disciple bond (Cole); "make you become" = gradual training (Bruce); "mending nets" = equipping, same word as Eph 4:12 (Lane, Strong).
4A (21-22): Capernaum synagogue remains; any qualified man could be invited to comment; scribes quoted authorities, Jesus taught with his own authority, from being sent by God; authority flows from submission to the Father.
4B (23-24): a person indwelt either by Christ or by an evil spirit (1 Cor 1:30); warns against spiritism, astrology, magic, drugs; the demon confesses him the Holy One, admitting the wilderness defeat.
4C (25-28): no ritual or formula, only command; Jesus silenced demons; Lane quotes Josephus on Eleazar the exorcist (roots, incantations, cup of water); Robertson: people used to incantations, this was different; fame spread.
4D (29-31): one sick woman in a private house; healed by touch and word, unlike Talmudic fever remedies (Barclay); she served at once: respond to blessing with service.
4E (32-34): after sunset, Sabbath over, the town brought the sick; many healed, demons not allowed to speak; Jesus put others' needs before his own.
5A (35): rose very early after a long day; Spurgeon: see God's face before man's; Poole: early morning and solitude help communion; he prayed to draw strength from the Father, not from weakness.
5B (36-39): disciples hunted for him and learned where he would be; refused to stay for popularity; preaching was central, miracles served the message.
5C (40): leprosy described; Lev 13-14 isolation and later traditions that made it harsher; the leper knelt and believed in his power; asked to be made clean, not only healed.
5D (41-45): moved with compassion; Luke 5:12 "full of leprosy"; he touched the untouchable and was not defiled but cleansed him; sent to the priest with Moses' offering (Lev 14; cedar, scarlet, hyssop like Num 19); disobedience spread the news and Jesus had to stay in desert places; obeying his word is better than our plans.
