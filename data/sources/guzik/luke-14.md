# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (English page), Luke 14
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/luke-14/ . Our own outline, not his text. Arabic page listed in search (arabic.enduringword.com/luke-14-ar/) redirects to the Arabic index; not readable.
A1 (1): Jesus kept company with Pharisees to love them; Barclay: "watching" = sinister espionage; Trapp: like a dog after a bone; we are letters read by others (2 Cor 3:2-3).
A2 (2-4): man with dropsy possibly planted; Clarke: malice and treachery; dropsy described (Liefeld, Barclay); Morgan: Jesus "answered" unspoken thoughts; no command forbids healing on Sabbath; human traditions over God's commands (Mark 7:8-9); healing immediate and complete.
A3 (5-6): ox or donkey in pit, how much more a person in God's image; Pate: either answer exposes them; Morgan: rebuke aimed to save, not shame.
B1 (7): parable = real-life illustration, not fable; guests competing for seats.
B2 (8-9): wedding feast central social event; humiliation risk; Ps 75:6-7.
B3 (10-11): take low seat contentedly; Phil 2:3; seeking low seat to be seen humble is subtle pride; George MacDonald: done without God fails or succeeds more miserably; Christ the example (Phil 2:5-11).
B4 (12-14): Geldenhuys: "do not habitually ask"; others-centered; Acts 20:35; repaid at resurrection of the just.
C1 (15): guest eases tension; messianic banquet, marriage supper of Lamb (Rev 19:9).
C2 (16-20): two-stage invitation, refusing after accepting an insult (Barclay); Spurgeon: excuses are curses, hope begins when excuses end; God has prepared everything; excuses foolish (field, oxen unseen); Morgan: behind every excuse is lack of desire; Spurgeon on "I cannot come".
C3 (21-24): poor, maimed, lame, blind; still room; highways and hedges; compel = God's desire to fill His house, overcoming disbelief by love; Bruce: they would doubt sincerity; Spurgeon: go to streets and markets; Barclay: Augustine and others misused "compel" (Inquisition) "the shame of Christianity"; Trapp quoting Perkins: magistrate and outward profession; Pate: Gentiles.
D1 (25-26): disciple = learner; accepting invitation not the whole; audacious claim, idolatry or madness if not God; Napoleon quote on empire founded on love; Spurgeon: "hate" comparative; good things the greatest idolatry threat.
D2 (27): Luke 9:23; condemned carried crossbeam; Barclay: Varus crucified about 2000 Jews along roads; Geldenhuys: not passive endurance but giving up self-seeking; Barclay: camp-follower without being a soldier; do not understate demands.
D3 (28-33): tower and king; can you afford to follow and to refuse; forsake = say goodbye; two extremes (clean up first / Jesus won't help after); opposing God costs more.
D4 (34-35): salt losing distinctiveness by corruption or assimilation; useful only with Christ's nature; ears to hear.
