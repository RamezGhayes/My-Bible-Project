# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), Titus 2
Read 2026-11-27 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/titus-2/ . Our own outline, not his text. Page subtitle: علمهم كيف يعيشون.
A1 (1): Titus contrasted with teachers of ch1; Clarke: few NT chapters surpass this; teach right living not only right thinking (Matt 28:19-20).
A2 (2): older men need wise patient teaching (young teacher may irritate); maturity not automatic with age; Hiebert: hupomone active endurance.
A3 (3-4a): reverent, not slanderers (1 Tim 3:11), not enslaved to much wine (Guthrie: Cretan setting, stronger term than 1 Tim 3:8); Guthrie: "teachers of good" unique compound.
A4 (4b-5): Titus does not teach young women directly, older women do; love husbands, children, sensible, pure, homemakers, submissive (Eph 5:22; Col 3:18); White: best test of a religion is its effect on adherents.
A5 (6): one instruction for young men: sensible; Barclay: sophron = will trained to master every impulse.
A6 (7-8): Titus as model; good works, integrity, sound speech; John 8:46; White: no evil to report, not nothing to say.
A7 (9-10): slaves submit, please, not talk back, not pilfer (Acts 5:2), faithful; Acts 5:29 limit; Spurgeon: Christian principles would undermine slavery; Draper: submit like soldiers ready for orders; adorn like setting jewels; Morgan: doctrine beautiful when its effects are seen.
B1 (11): grace brings salvation, offered to all, no separate gospel for some; White: no class beyond reach of saving grace; Clarke: epiphaino like sun shining on all (Mal 4:2).
B2 (12-13): grace as teacher (Hiebert); Calvin: proclaiming grace must lead to godly life; Spurgeon: hardest part of training the young is uprooting the wrong; blessed hope = Jesus Himself, Clarke rejects purgatory; White: "great" applied to the true God (contrast Acts 19:28).
B3 (14): Christ gave Himself voluntarily as substitute; redeem from all iniquity; Barclay: periousios like spoils a king reserves for himself; zealous for good works.
B4 (15): speak, exhort, rebuke with all authority; let no one despise; Hiebert: applies to Titus as much as to Cretans.
