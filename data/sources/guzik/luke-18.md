# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), Luke 18
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/luke-18/ . Our own outline, not his text.
A1 (1): purpose stated: pray and not lose heart; prayer instinct (Eccl 3:11) meets obstacles; continual attitude, not nonstop kneeling (1 Thess 5:17); prayer is labour (Col 4:12; Heb 7:25); Morrison: prayer engages mind, heart, will; Spurgeon on persistent asking; examples Canaanite woman, Jacob, Rachel.
A2 (2-8): Barclay: judge likely a non-Jewish appointee of bad reputation; Clarke and Pate: "wear me out" a boxing image, blow under the eye (1 Cor 9:27); God unlike the judge, just and eager; persistence changes us, not God; persecuted believers need it; Spurgeon: child knocking without waiting; Pate: Jesus and Paul repeated prayers (Mark 14:39; 2 Cor 12:8) but beware repetition from doubt; will He find faith?
B1 (9-14): target those trusting themselves; Pharisee prays about himself (Pate: own agenda); Clarke: Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai claimed he and his son among the righteous; Barclay: Monday/Thursday fasts on market days; Morris: right about himself, on the wrong road; Spurgeon on breast-beating; word for mercy related to atonement (Heb 2:17); Barclay: "the sinner"; humility = seeing oneself truly (Prov 3:34; Jas 4:6; 1 Pet 5:5).
B2 (15-17): Barclay: mothers brought children on first birthday for a rabbi's blessing; Spurgeon: Jesus blessed, did not baptize them; Pate: children model citizens by love and trust; receive kingdom as a child, without earning.
C1 (18-19): Plummer/Geldenhuys: Talmud has no rabbi called "good teacher"; Jesus not denying His goodness but challenging the man's view; Spurgeon: argument implies Jesus is God.
C2 (20-21): commandments about neighbours; Pate: "from my youth" = bar mitzvah at about 13; doubtful he kept them fully; Paul "blameless" (Phil 3:6).
C3 (22-23): sell all, give, follow; tests love for God above possessions (Deut 6:5); command specific to this man, danger of riches general; Mark 10:21 Jesus loved him; Trapp: do not think poverty worse than hell; Francis of Assisi example.
C4 (24-27): hard for rich; Morris: no need to explain away camel and needle, humorous image; Barclay: Jewish saying of elephant through needle; France: wealth as sign of blessing overturned; Carson: rich can be saved (Abraham); Morgan: only God enables entry; Zacchaeus, Joseph of Arimathea, Barnabas; 1 Tim 6:9-10.
C5 (28-30): reward many times now and eternal life; spiritual not material; Matthew Poole: joy, contentment, provision, sometimes restoration like Job; generosity protects from wealth (Ps 62:10).
C6 (31-34): third prediction; Spurgeon on the humiliation; Geldenhuys: expectation of triumphant Messiah overshadowed suffering Messiah; meaning hidden, perhaps because they could not yet bear it.
C7 (35-39): blind beggar near Jericho cries "Son of David"; Barclay: the verb shows rising desperate shouting; asks mercy, claims no right; Matthew 20:29 / Mark 10:46 Bartimaeus; two Jerichos (old and Roman) explain leaving/approaching.
C8 (40-43): Jesus stops and asks what he wants; invitation to voice needs (Jas 4:2); faith seen in seeking Jesus, recognising Him, calling Him Lord; follows and glorifies God.
