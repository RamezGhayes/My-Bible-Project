# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word, Acts 12 (Arabic)
URL read: https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/acts-12/ (Arabic, read 2026-11-27 via WebFetch, two per-verse outlines).
A1 (1): Herod Agrippa I, grandson of Herod the Great, nephew of Antipas; political popularity; politicians persecuting for popularity.
A2 (2): after successes (Saul, Cornelius, Antioch) opposition returns; first of Twelve martyred, not first martyr (Stephen); no special protection (Matt 10:16-26); Mark 10:35-40 cup and baptism; John fulfilled differently by tradition; sword = beheaded; Eusebius 2.9.2-3 citing Clement: guard converted and died with James; not replaced, unlike Judas.
B1 (3-4): political motive vs Saul's sincere misguided zeal (8:1-3); Days of Unleavened Bread; timing after Passover; Horton three reasons; heavy guard due to earlier escape (5:17-21); Stott: soldier on each side; Hughes citing Seneca: two chained, two at door.
B2 (5): Herod fights with soldiers, church with prayer; heaven's door open; ektenos stretched muscle (cites MacArthur, medical term); Luke 22:44; unanswered prayer often not fervent; fervent prayer shows we care what God cares (John 15:7); addressed to God.
B3 (6-11): sleeping, Ps 127:2; chains nothing to God; obeyed without understanding; iron gate: God deals with it when we arrive; automate = by itself; Bruce: Sundar Singh well rescue; James not rescued: Peter's time not yet, James glorified not loser.
B4 (12-17): went to praying people; Rhoda detail = historical reliability; "his angel" Jewish guardian-angel belief; fervent but weak faith; James brother of the Lord; Peter leaves narrative except Acts 15; Gal 2:11-14; his letters.
B5 (18-19): guards executed, common practice.
C1 (20-21): Tyre and Sidon depended on king's land for food, would pay any price; Blastus; Herod addresses favor-seeking crowd.
C2 (22-23): people seek political saviors; Herod enjoyed honor; struck because he did not give glory; worms fit inner corruption; Josephus: silver robe at dawn, cried god, stomach pains, died five days later.
C3 (24-25): Herod judged, church blessed; killed James, jailed Peter, did not stop God; examples of those who fought God (Nietzsche, Sinclair Lewis, Hemingway); Barnabas, Saul return (11:30), John Mark.
