# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), Luke 4
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/luke-4/ . Our own outline, not his text.
A1 (1-2a): full of the Spirit, linked to baptism (3:21-22); depends on Father and Spirit rather than on his divine prerogatives; Heb 4:15; temptation harder for one who never yields; three senses of "tempt" (Satan tempts us, we test God, God tests us; Jas 1:13-14; 1 Cor 10:9; Heb 11:17); Barclay: story must come from Jesus himself; Spirit leads into wilderness too; Adam tested in ideal conditions, Jesus in harsh ones.
A2 (2b-4): truly hungry after forty days; 1 Cor 10:13; "if" = "since"; legitimate desire by illegitimate means; first Adam fell by eating (Gen 3:6); Deut 8:3; Barclay: limestone stones like loaves; resist with Spirit and truth.
A3 (5-8): vision of kingdoms; offer of the world without the cross; Satan's authority not disputed (John 12:31; Eph 2:2); suffering then glory (Luke 24:25-26; Rev 11:15); "Get behind/Go, Satan" with Jas 4:7; Deut 6:13; Morgan: completeness of temptation measures the victory.
A4 (9-13): high pinnacle; Geldenhuys citing Pesiqta Rabbati (Messiah revealed on the temple roof); Ps 91:11-12 twisted out of context; Deut 6:16; Acts 20:27; 2 Tim 2:15; Morris: spectacle for amazement not God's purpose; "for a time"; Word without Spirit = pride, Spirit without Word = excess.
B1 (14-15): power of the Spirit; Josephus: 240 towns of Galilee, at least 15,000 each; Clarke: God turns temptations to good; synagogue teaching the focus.
B2 (16-17): Nazareth, custom of synagogue attendance; carpentry for townspeople; Matt 4:12-13; synagogue order (prayer, Law, Prophets, sermon by visiting teacher).
B3 (18-19): anointed one = Messiah; anointing of priests; each phrase tied to an effect of sin; Morrison: Christ embodies liberty; Jubilee (Lev 25:9-15), Pate: return may have fallen in a Jubilee year; stops before "day of vengeance" (Isa 61:2), still future.
B4 (20-22): sat down to teach, eyes fixed; "today" answers who and when; marvelled at gracious words yet "Joseph's son?".
B5 (23-27): "Physician, heal yourself"; Spurgeon: Nazarenes thought they had a claim on his power; Pate: expected him to serve his own first; Elijah and Zarephath widow (1 Kgs 17), Elisha and Naaman (2 Kgs 5); rejection reveals hearts and God works in unexpected places; Naaman's obedience, humility, faith.
B6 (28-30): rage; brow of the hill; throwing down often first step of stoning; Trapp: a second Samson; passed through them, avoiding the cliff of the temptation.
C1 (31-37): authority in Capernaum; demon knows the Holy One of God while hometown does not; rebuke, no harm; Pate: demon feared kingdom ending demonic rule; Pate: contrast with noisy exorcists.
C2 (38-39): Peter married; Clement of Alexandria on Peter's wife; rebuked fever; Barclay: Luke's medical wording "great fever"; Spurgeon: her immediate service shows complete healing.
C3 (40-41): at sunset, hands on each one; demons silenced; Gospels name fewer than ten exorcism cases.
C4 (42-44): lonely place, crowds seek him; must preach in other towns; preaching the purpose, miracles support it.
