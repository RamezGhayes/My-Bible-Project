# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (English page), 1 Corinthians 6
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/1-corinthians-6/ . Our own outline, not his text. (No Arabic page found by search.)
A1 (1): "Dare any of you" shows Paul's shock. Greek love of litigation made lawsuits public. Bema = magistrate's seat in the marketplace. "Unrighteous" is religious (unjustified, unsaved), not a claim the judges were corrupt.
A2 (2-6): Saints will judge the world and (evil) angels with Christ, so they can settle small matters. Corinthians proud of wisdom (1:18-31) yet no wise man among them. Paul himself appealed to Roman courts for his rights (Acts 22:25; 25:10-11), but brother must not sue brother. Settle in church or Christian arbitration; criminal matters belong to the state (Rom 13:3-4). Paul does not address suing unbelievers. Quotes G. Campbell Morgan (passage a far-reaching statement about the saints' union with their Lord) and Adam Clarke (those refusing arbitration by brethren should be put out of the church).
A3 (7): Suing a brother is already utter failure; the Corinthians loved their "rights". Better to be wronged and cheated than to damage God's glory; no one who accepts wrong for God's sake truly loses. If church fails, trust God not pagan courts.
A4 (8-11): The wrongdoer defrauded brothers; dishonesty among Christians drives people away. Paul still calls him brother but rejects faith detached from conduct. The list describes lives characterized by these sins, yet a lifestyle begins with single acts. Malakoi literally male prostitutes; arsenokoitai generic for homosexual practice. Do not excuse it nor single it out. Nero and Sporus as background. "Such were some of you". Washed (Titus 3:5; Acts 22:16; Rev 1:5; Eph 5:26), sanctified (Heb 10:10; John 17:19; Acts 26:18; Rom 15:16), justified, declared righteous not merely not guilty (Rom 3:24, 28); Matt 1:21. Quotes Gordon Fee: security in Christ becomes false security if it ignores the "such were" warning.
B1 (12): "All things are lawful" probably Paul's own teaching on liberty (Col 2:16-17) misapplied by Corinthians to sex. Not all things helpful. "Brought under the power" same verb as 7:4 (spouse's authority over body); may mean "any body", e.g. a harlot's. Use of prostitutes culturally accepted in Corinth.
B2 (13-14): Slogan "foods for the stomach" used to justify every appetite. Food and sex are not alike: body's dependence on food passes, but the body will be raised, so what we do with it morally matters. Consequences such as pregnancies and disease.
B3 (15-17): "Do you not know" - they did not connect sexual conduct with Christ. Whole body of Christ disgraced. Sex makes one flesh, blessed in marriage, under curse outside. No "casual sex" in the Bible; part of self given away. Lust often a desire for love and acceptance, met in being one spirit with the Lord. Quotes Gordon Fee (making someone outside Christ unlawful lord over one's body) and Wiersbe (sex outside marriage like robbing a bank; inside like a deposit that pays dividends).
B4 (18): Flee, do not resist or test yourself; Joseph (Gen 39:7-21). Porneia broad, includes pornography and gratification short of intercourse. Sex in marriage holy (Heb 13:4). Sexual sin not necessarily worse but uniquely affects the body. Quotes Adam Clarke: if you parley you are undone; do not reason, fly.
B5 (19-20): Body a temple of the Spirit; in ch. 3 (3:16) the church is the temple, here the individual. Bought, so not ours. Extends to idleness and waste. Indwelling Spirit gives power, higher standard. He denies a Christian can be demon possessed: Christ does not sublet. Many old manuscripts end with "glorify God in your body". Quotes Spurgeon (body once the devil's willing horse should not be a sluggish hack pulling Christ's chariot) and Harry Ironside (glorify God in your body and the spiritual side will take care of itself).
