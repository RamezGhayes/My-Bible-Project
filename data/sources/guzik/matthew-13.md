# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), Matthew 13
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/matthew-13/ . Our own outline, not his text. (Some cross-reference numbers on the page extract looked garbled; only clear ones used.)
A1 (1-3a): teaching from boat; shift to open air; parable = story with teaching, one main point; Barclay (not allegory), Spurgeon, France, Poole (memory); Mark 4:1.
A2 (3b-9): sowing before plowing; four soils; ears to hear.
A3 (10-17): reveal to receptive, conceal from hardened; whoever has gets more; Isaiah 6 fulfilled; sun softens wax, hardens clay; Barclay, Carson, Spurgeon; disciples blessed.
A4 (18-23): same seed and sower, difference in soil; Spurgeon (Word judges hearers), Trapp (hardened by overexposure), France (collapse under pressure), Poole; encouragement; examine soil.
B1 (24-30): corruption among God's people; darnel poisonous, indistinguishable till heading (France); separation delayed (Carson).
B2 (31-32): most see church growth (Clarke, Bruce; France: empire image Ezek 31, Dan 4); Guzik reads as abnormal growth/corruption; birds evil (Morgan, Carson).
B3 (33): leaven usually sin; large quantity (France ~40 litres, 100 people); Barclay surprising; Morgan pagan influences.
B4 (34-35): only parables in this period (Carson: regular part); Ps 78:2; hidden things = church revealed later by Paul.
B5 (36-43): field = world not church; angels separate; Clarke (flood of grace met by flood of iniquity); Spurgeon (church may remove open sinners, not judge hearts); France; Carson (Augustine vs Donatists); fire; righteous shine.
C1 (44): man = Jesus buying the field (world) to gain his people; Carson (rabbinic law, hyperbole); Spurgeon (bought world at highest price).
C2 (45-46): merchant = Jesus, believer = pearl; Barclay pearl loveliest possession.
C3 (47-50): world stays divided to the end; angels separate; France: division of humanity, not mainly mixed church.
C4 (51-52): did they understand? scribe trained = householder; Poole (Ezra 7:6, both testaments), Spurgeon (not weary of old, not afraid of new), Barclay, Clarke; ministers skilled in Scripture.
D1 (53-56): normal upbringing vs apocryphal infancy tales (France); carpenter's son; Justin Martyr via Trapp (ploughs and yokes); Spurgeon on Julian the Apostate; brothers and sisters vs perpetual virginity; Clarke.
D2 (57-58): offended; familiarity; unbelief limits works, God works in partnership with faith; Trapp: unbelief palsies the hand of omnipotence.
