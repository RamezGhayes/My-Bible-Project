# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), Luke 16
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/luke-16/ (Arabic page exists; title "المال والبر"). Our own outline, not his text.
A1 (1-8): steward accused of wasting, asked for account, reduces bills; Barclay: chose embezzlement as career; master praises shrewdness not dishonesty; God can use wicked example (like Paul's war/slavery images); two lessons (took accounting seriously, used present to prepare future); Spurgeon: account for time, gifts, influence; Maclaren: devil's servants should not out-work Christ's.
A2 (9): use worldly resources for eternity; Pate: mammon Aramaic, negative sense; Clarke: riches promise much, deliver little; 1992 survey on wanting a bit more income.
A3 (10-12): faithfulness with money shows trustworthiness for spiritual things; leaders must manage money well (not be rich); Meyer: God tests with money.
A4 (13): two masters vs two jobs; divided heart; money shows love for God; 1990 LA businessman killed holding expensive watch; shopping as comfort; Morgan: money a curse to its servant; Trapp: worldly wisdom does not get one to heaven.
A5 (14-15): money-loving Pharisees mock; Barclay: body language; self-justification before men; God knows hearts; esteemed by men detestable to God.
A6 (16-18): law and prophets until John; "presses in": common reading (zealots forcing kingdom; spiritual warfare) and Pate (evil forces opposing Jesus' ministry); law fulfilled not abolished; divorce: adultery (Matt 5:31-32; 19:9), desertion by unbeliever (1 Cor 7:15); otherwise adultery; rabbis allowed trivial reasons; don't build doctrine on one statement.
B1 (19-21): likely a real account since only here a character is named; Barclay: food, wiping hands with bread; "Dives"; neglect not cruelty; Morrison: physically close, socially far apart.
B2 (22-23): Pate: three senses of Abraham's bosom (Gen 15:15; 25:8; John 1:18; 13:23); not saved by poverty nor lost by wealth; Hades = Sheol, place of the dead with two areas (rest/torment) before the cross; Hades a waiting place until Rev 20:11-13; since the cross believers go to the Lord (2 Cor 5:6-8); reasonable that Christ went to Hades, preached and freed captives (Acts 2:24-31; 1 Pet 3:18-19; Eph 4:8; Isa 61:1); Gehenna = Hinnom (2 Chr 28:3; Jer 32:35), lake of fire prepared for devil (Matt 25:41); Trapp: torment without pity. Theology note added in voices (two-compartment Hades / descent not the usual Reformed reading).
B3 (24-26): still treats Lazarus as servant; Morrison: recognized Lazarus, suffering from desires not knowledge; sin = life without love for God; Pate: no hint of purgatory; fixed chasm.
B4 (27-31): five brothers; Moses and the Prophets; Spurgeon: no sign convinces one who refuses Scripture; Lazarus better as witness from grave than preacher (Spurgeon); Morgan: Scripture powerful in itself; John 11 raising of Lazarus and leaders' response; Morrison: Pharisees and rich man both neglected the needy in opposite ways.
