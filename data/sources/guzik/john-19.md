# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), John 19
Read 2026-11-27 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/john-19/ (two WebFetch passes: full outline, then detail on 1-3, 11, 14-16, 25-27, 30, 34, 38-42). Our own outline, not his text.
A1 (1-4 on page; we use 1-3): Pilate scourges a man he called faultless (18:38), perhaps to appease the crowd; Roman scourge of thongs tipped with bone or metal, victim tied to a low post, many died, Barclay: some lost their minds; three purposes (punishment, confession, weakening before crucifixion); Morris: Gospels describe it in one word, no appeal to emotion; thorns long and sharp, purple costly and royal, mock salute; Matthew adds reed, kneeling, spitting (Matt 27:27-30; 26:67-68); Spurgeon: do the opposite of the soldiers, honor the King sincerely.
A1/A2 (4-6): Clarke counts five attempts by Pilate to release Jesus (Luke 23:4, 15, 20, 22; John 19:4, 12, 13); "Behold the man": Trench (crown stays on), Spurgeon (crown of thorns vs crowns won by others' blood), Tasker, Trapp (if they are men they should pity him), Morgan; v6 Tenney: Pilate mocks the Sanhedrin, who could not execute.
A3 (7-9): charge "Son of God" = claim to deity (Clarke); Pilate more afraid (Tasker on the strength of the word); Morris: Romans knew tales of gods in human form; "where are you from" the right question (Tasker); Jesus already said his kingdom is not of this world (18:36).
A4 (10-11): emphatic pronoun (Tasker); silence = Isa 53:7; authority from above, Rome thought it had final power; greater sin: Judas or Caiaphas, deliberately vague (Tasker); paradidomai used for Judas (Bruce); Pilate not sinless, only less guilty; Matt 27:24.
A5 (12-13): "friend of Caesar" a formal title (Tenney); Morris: this threat settled the case; Boice: Pilate's insecure position tied to the emperor's circle; Matt 27:19; Gabbatha from gabah, high place (Clarke).
A6 (14-16): sixth hour vs Mark 15:25: Westcott (Roman reckoning from midnight), Morris (approximate times), Alford (copyist confusion of numerals); Passover preparation and Lamb of God (1:29); "Behold your King" sarcasm foreshadowing the title (Alford); "no king but Caesar": Maclaren, rejecting Christ means bowing to a tyrant; Pilate the weak man who sells conscience for self-interest.
B1 (17-18): patibulum (Bruce); Isaac carrying wood (Dods); Golgotha place of the skull; outside the gate (Heb 13:12); between two, humanity represented (Dods, Morris); Clarke and others: Jesus "in the middle".
B2 (19-22): Roman custom of posting the charge (Tenney); three languages (Tenney, Trapp); "what I have written" (Clarke on unchangeable sentence).
B3 (23-24): Artemidorus, Mishnah Sanhedrin 6:3 on nakedness; seamless tunic recalls the high priest's robe (Exod 28:31-32); Ps 22:18; 2 Cor 8:9; soldiers unaware (Tenney).
B4 (25-27): four women; sister = Salome (Morris; Mark 15:40; Matt 27:56); sword of Luke 2:35; "Woman" respectful, Clarke: avoided "mother" to spare her grief; brothers not yet believers (Matt 12:46-47; 13:55-56; John 2:12; 7:3-10); possible reasons listed for entrusting her to John (kingdom ties over blood ties, John present, brothers unbelieving, John would outlive others, plain wisdom); Spurgeon: right relationship produces right action. Page does not discuss Mary as mother of believers.
B5 (28-30): "I thirst" fulfils Ps 69:21; sour wine on hyssop, unlike the drugged wine refused (Mark 15:23; Tasker); Barclay: hyssop and Passover; tetelestai: shout of victory (Spurgeon, Morris), used on receipts "paid in full" (Tenney); what was finished: prophecies and types, sacrifices, obedience, justice satisfied, power of Satan, sin, death; Meyer: end of blood sacrifices; Spurgeon: we work from gratitude, not to earn; bowed head = laying head to rest (Bruce, Matt 8:20); gave up his spirit (John 10:17-18; Augustine, Trench); non-biblical witnesses to his death (Mara bar Serapion, Josephus, Tacitus, Talmud).
C1 (31-32): high Sabbath; Spurgeon on religious hypocrisy; Tenney: breaking legs stopped breathing, quick death.
C2 (33-34): centurion confirms (Mark 15:44-45); spear to confirm death (Dods); blood and water: ruptured heart and pericardial fluid suggested, flow unusual; OT cleansing; Spurgeon: Eve from Adam's side, church from Christ's side; 1 John 5:6 uncertain.
C3 (35-37): eyewitness so that we believe; Exod 12:46; Num 9:12; Ps 34:20; Zech 12:10 looks to the future (Trench).
C4 (38-42): Joseph secret disciple, rich (Matt 27:57); Roman custom of leaving bodies (Morris, Dods); Nicodemus about 100 Roman pounds (~34-35 kg) (Tenney, Dods); Isa 53:9; new garden tomb, ossuaries and rolling stones (Dods); garden: Adam fell in a garden, second Adam redeems in one (Tasker), Morrison; Spurgeon: Achilles and Hector, unused tomb; points: burial confirms death, the secret disciples come out, disciples' faith tested, burial with Christ in baptism, Matt 12:40, Ps 16:10, 1 Cor 15:3-4; 1 Pet 3:18-20 read as preaching to spirits in prison during the time in the tomb.
Theology note: 19:38-42 Guzik reads 1 Pet 3:18-20 as Christ preaching to the spirits during his time in the tomb; neutral note added (many Reformed interpreters read it as Christ preaching by the Spirit in Noah's day).
Second pass 2026-11-27 (owner asked for per-verse depth): page re-read per verse in halves; extra points used in entries (e.g. 19: Cicero, Tacitus, 1968 find, Nico Haas, William Edwards, Lactantius, Apuleius; 20: Morgan/Trench on peace, Maclaren on repeating words; 21: Trapp, Plummer, Bruce, Boice's invitations, exetasai, Spurgeon on empty nets, Tenney on 'you').
