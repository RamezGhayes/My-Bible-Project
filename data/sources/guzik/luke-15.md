# Reading notes: David Guzik, Enduring Word (Arabic page), Luke 15
Read 2026-11-26 at https://enduringword.com/bible-commentary/ar/luke-15/ (Arabic page exists; title "الفرح بإيجاد المفقود"). Our own outline, not his text.
A1 (1-3): hard discipleship teaching (14:25) drew sinners; Morris: leaders split people into unclean and righteous and avoided the unclean; Pate: rabbinic advice not to associate with the wicked.
A2 (4-7): joy of recovery, not neglect of 99; Clarke: sheep strays easily, bleats as it runs off; common view (God accepts those who come) vs Jesus (God seeks); Barclay: Jewish scholar called seeking God new; shoulders linked to Christ dying for sinners (page cites Rom 5:6 / 6:5, garbled); heaven's joy.
A3 (8-10): Bruce: coin perhaps part of married woman's silver headdress; Spurgeon: still her coin when lost; lamp/sweeping = Spirit-led church seeking souls; Morrison: worth of each individual; Isa 62:5; Zeph 3:17; coin cannot repent, so Jesus adds the repentant sinner.
B1 (11-16): Geldenhuys: inheritance before or usually after death; request from folly and greed; father = God's love respecting freedom; far country, pleasurable at first; famine not his fault; pigs unclean (Lev 11:7; page has 7:11).
B2 (17-19): "came to himself"; Morrison: not his true self in rebellion; blamed no one, looked to father; Barclay: servant part of household vs hired man dismissible; "give me" vs "make me".
B3 (20-24): saw from afar, ran (unusual for his age/culture), kissed repeatedly; speech interrupted; robe, ring, sandals = honor, not need; Spurgeon: son did not bargain or give notice, just left; Pate: depth of repentance matched by depth of father's love.
B4 (25-32): elder son working in field; angry, refused; father pleads; complaint; Morrison: enjoyed daily fellowship without valuing it; Morris: self-righteous always feel unappreciated; Morgan: lived in father's house without understanding father's heart; answers the Pharisees' grumbling; three parables show seeking Son, Spirit through church, Father; Spurgeon: grace not merit.
