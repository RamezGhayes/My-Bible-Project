## mark-john-baptist: "John the Baptist", 2005-11-13, Mark 1:1-8
https://learn.ligonier.org/sermons/mark-john-baptist
- Opens by picturing persecuted Roman believers under Nero hearing Mark read aloud.
- Author John Mark, once parted from Paul and later reconciled; Papias, Eusebius, Irenaeus: Mark as Peter's interpreter. Audience Roman Christians after the fire of AD 64; date about AD 65.
- Mark is a "witness document", no birth narrative. Greek euthys ("immediately") 42 times in Mark vs about 12 elsewhere in NT; anecdote of a mission boat named Euthys.
- Mark created the gospel genre; kata in the titles ("according to"). v1 states the theme, Jesus is Christ and Son of God, the two halves of Peter's confession at Caesarea Philippi.
- v2-3: Mark blends texts from Exodus, Malachi, Isaiah (no verse numbers given). Herald is not the Messiah. Empty chair for Elijah at Passover; Malachi's closing promise. John denies being Elijah (John 1:21) yet Jesus calls him Elijah: he came in Elijah's spirit and power.
- Wilderness: place where God met prophets (Moses at the bush, exodus, Elijah fed by ravens).
- v4-5: baptism of repentance was radical (to be treated later); John became a celebrity because prophecy was thought to have ceased. Pharisees: cleansing is for Gentiles, we are Abraham's children; answer: God can raise children of Abraham from stones.
- v6: real camel hair and leather belt, like Elijah; Roman readers would recall Nero dressing Christians in animal skins for dogs. Locusts and honey mentioned only.
- v7-8: untying sandals a slave's task (dirty feet, aristocrats did not do it). Water vs Holy Spirit baptism, the Spirit of power. From other Gospels: fan in hand, axe at the root.
- Close: the catacomb believers were strengthened to endure beasts and fire.

## mark-baptism-and-temptation-jesus: "The Baptism and Temptation of Jesus", 2005-11-20, Mark 1:9-13
https://learn.ligonier.org/sermons/mark-baptism-and-temptation-jesus
- v9: John objects; Jesus: it is fitting to fulfill all righteousness. Doctrine: vicarious, substitutionary life; Jesus as second Adam who must live perfect obedience, not only die; his righteousness credited to believers. Child's answer "Jesus died for my sins" true but half the gospel.
- v10: Trinitarian event. The Spirit's descent does not make Jesus divine (Logos fully God from conception); the Spirit anoints his human nature for his mission and miracles.
- v11: one of three times the Father's voice is heard audibly in NT. Fourfold witness: John, opened heavens, visible Spirit, Father's voice.
- v12: "immediately" the Spirit drove him: compulsion, not suggestion. Wilderness motif again.
- Adam vs Jesus: lush garden, food, companion vs desert, alone, fasting forty days; same test: will you believe and obey God's word ("Has God said").
- v13: three temptations (from the other Gospels), each answered by Scripture; "If you are the Son of God" right after the Father had said "You are my Son". Satan leaves for a season. With wild beasts; angels serve. Application to Christians facing beasts in the arena.

## mark-beginning-jesus-public-ministry: "Beginning of Jesus' Public Ministry", 2005-11-27, Mark 1:14-20
https://learn.ligonier.org/sermons/mark-beginning-jesus-public-ministry
- Christianity is bound to real history, not myth; redemptive history is still history.
- v14: textual variant "gospel of the kingdom of God" / "gospel of God"; meaning the same; no claim of inspiration for copies. Gospel belongs to God (cf. Rom 1:1). Gospel: genre, coming of the kingdom, person and work of Christ. God always reigns, but the promise was God visiting the world through Messiah. John in prison; Jesus takes up his message in Galilee.
- v15: chronos vs kairos (Oscar Cullmann, Christ and Time); historical vs historic events. "Fulfilled": pleroma, overflowing fullness, glass filled to the brim. Kairos moments: exodus, birth, death, resurrection of Christ.
- "At hand": near in time and space, the King is present. Krisis = judgment: receive him, life; reject, judgment. Repent and believe: criticism of evangelism offering purpose without repentance, "cheap grace".
- v16-18: Sea of Galilee about 13 by 7 miles; Josephus: Romans seized about 250 boats in AD 68. Disciples were business owners, not poor. Casting net vs drag net. Command "follow me", fishers of men; they leave at once.
- v19-20: James and John leave Zebedee with hired men.
- Disciple (mathetes) = learner under a rabbi; Twelve a subset of larger group (at least seventy). Students usually chose rabbis; Jesus chose his. Walking teacher like Aristotle's Lyceum; memorization preserved his sayings. Disciples also served the teacher.
- Application: Peter Marshall's story of a fishmonger who left his shop; every Christian is called to follow and serve.

## mark-healing-man-unclean: "Healing of the Man with the Unclean Spirit", 2005-12-04, Mark 1:21-28
https://learn.ligonier.org/sermons/mark-healing-man-unclean
(reads 1:21-31 but expounds 21-28; 29-31 in next sermon)
- Capernaum: NW shore, prosperous, seawall and piers, fishing, friendly Roman presence; Jesus' base, probably Peter's house. Name "village of Nahum". Synagogue needs ten men; an assembly, not the temple; visiting teachers. Fourth-century synagogue in imported white limestone instead of black basalt.
- v22: astonishment bordering on fear. Scribes quoted authorities; Jesus quoted none, spoke as God speaks, like the prophets. exousia = ex + ousia, speaking out of the very being of God; link to Nicene homoousios. Apply: receive Scripture with awe.
- v23-26: possession rare in OT, intense opposition during Jesus' ministry. "Us" may speak for Satan's kingdom. Nothing in common, only conflict; demons know their judgment. Naming a foe to gain control (Jacob asks the angel's name). Irony: in OT "holy one of God" used of Samson, the strong man; Jesus binds the strong man. Holiness terrifies sinners; compare Peter's "depart from me". Rebuke blunt like "shut up". Convulsion and exit.
- v27-28: new teaching; not a magician, no props, only a word. Finger of God = Spirit; kingdom has come. Fame spreads.

## mark-jesus-heals-many: "Jesus Heals Many", 2005-12-11, Mark 1:29-45
https://learn.ligonier.org/sermons/mark-jesus-heals-many
- v29-31: Peter's house; excavations: fourth-century synagogue on first-century foundation; nearby house with graffiti and meeting space widely held to be Peter's house and an early church. Peter likely Mark's source. Peter was married (remark on the papacy; Sproul notes he too is married).
- v32-34: after sunset; "whole city" hyperbole; demons silenced because they knew him.
- v35-37: before dawn, solitary prayer; Mark records three night prayers of Jesus. Verb "searched" implies a hunt; "everyone seeks you" a veiled rebuke.
- v38-39: mission over popularity; crowds wanted healing not truth. We seek God in sickness but neglect his Word.
- v40-42: about 72 skin diseases called leprosy; maybe Hansen's. Lev 13:1-6 read, Lev 13-14 recommended: priest's inspection, white hair, deeper than skin, seven-day quarantine. Leper excluded from community, temple, city; torn clothes, covered lip, cry "unclean", about fifty paces. Leper breaks law approaching; Jesus breaks ceremonial law by touch: Lord over ceremonial law (traffic officer overriding a red light). Word with power like "Lazarus, come forth" and creation by speech. "Compassion" may be read as "angry": indignation against disease. Rabbis: healing leprosy harder than raising dead.
- v43-44: messianic secret: expectation of political deliverer vs Suffering Servant; crowds would hinder mission.
- v45: disobedient evangelist; Jesus stays in deserted places. Close: come to Christ for his message, not only relief; Christmas.
